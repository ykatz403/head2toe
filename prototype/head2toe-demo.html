    Monetization

    Yes, it can earn. The money is in the click to buy, not the paywall.

    Your three questions, answered up front. Figures are typical industry ranges to sanity-check against real affiliate networks before you present them as forecasts.

    Will people pay a subscription?Some will, for extras, not for browsing.

    Freemium apps usually convert a low single-digit share of active users. Keep the core studio free and charge about $4–8 a month for the 3D scan, a saved wardrobe and unlimited looks.

    Ads on the page?Possible, but they'd cost you the look.

    Standard display ads pay roughly $2–10 per thousand views and make a premium page look like a coupon site. The classy version is a labelled "Sponsored" slot, where a brand pays to appear in shuffles.

    Money from people buying?That's the main engine.

    Retail affiliate programs typically pay about 3–15% of each sale, and the user pays nothing extra. You already send buyers with intent. This is the strongest fit for the concept.

    Reality check

    Three things to settle before you build the real one.

    Scraping brand photos is the weak link

    Copying retailer images breaks their terms and is a copyright risk, and a flat product photo doesn't wrap onto a body convincingly anyway. Use affiliate product feeds instead, which include images you are licensed to promote. Then either use 3D garment models or an AI try-on model to place the item on the avatar. Many luxury houses don't run their own affiliate programs, so link to retailers that stock them (Farfetch, Mytheresa, SSENSE, Nordstrom) rather than the brand site.

    Getting the "you" in 3D

    Start with a guided phone capture (a few photos or a short video) that produces an avatar, using an existing avatar or body-scan SDK rather than building one. That's what this demo stands in for with the height, build and skin-tone controls. Fit is the harder half: showing that a size medium actually fits you needs garment size data from the retailer.

    Body scans are sensitive data

    Treat scans as biometric data. GDPR in Europe and laws like Illinois' BIPA in the US require explicit consent, clear deletion and careful storage. Doing this well is also a selling point: "your scan never leaves your device" is a feature people will pay for.

    • Stand about 2 m (6 ft) from your phone, propped up or held by a friend.
